CRITICAL — rules for beats

### 1. Overall structure

- beats is an array of exactly 5 full-fledged scenes for every chapter.
- Each scene is a separate dramatic episode with its own development of events.
- Each scene is a JSON object with four fields:
  - scene — the scene's ordinal number, from 1 to 5.
  - word_target — the target length of prose for this scene.
  - action_chain — a sequence of 4–6 concrete micro-events.
  - result — the scene's outcome and the change in the situation relative to its start.
- Do not turn scenes into chapter summaries. Each scene must contain enough
  event material to later be written as a full dramatic episode.

### 2. Rules for action_chain

- Every action_chain item describes a concrete action, interaction, reaction,
  decision, or consequence.
- Micro-events are placed in strict chronological order.
- Adjacent micro-events must show a cause-and-effect link: the preceding
  action, the reaction to it, and the situation that arises next.
- Every chain must include concrete character actions, interactions between
  them, obstacles, attempts to change the state of affairs, and the
  consequences of decisions made.
- Do not merely state what happened. Show the sequence of actions that led
  to the event.
- Where necessary, include changes of tactics, clashes of interest,
  resistance from other characters, unexpected circumstances, and the use of
  objects or resources.
- Dialogue must not be written out verbatim. State what the characters argue
  about, agree on, conceal, what information they try to obtain or pass on,
  and how the conversation affects further actions.
- Micro-events must give the writer model enough material to expand what is
  happening into full episodes, adding literary description, dialogue,
  inner reactions, and intermediate actions.
- Do not merge several unrelated events into one item.
- Do not repeat the same action reworded to inflate the volume.
- Do not invent artificial conflicts and random obstacles just to fill a scene.
- Not every micro-event needs a conflict. Preparation, investigation,
  observation, calm interaction, and reflection on consequences are allowed
  as long as they serve a plot function.

Bad example:

'Victor realizes the situation is becoming dangerous.'

Good example:

'Victor tries to open the staff terminal with his access card but is denied.
He notices an archive employee closely watching his attempts and stops, so as
not to draw extra attention.'

Bad example:

'The characters discuss the further plan.'

Good example:

'Maria suggests going to the department head, but Victor objects: an official
request would reveal their interest in the archive documents. He proposes
checking the registration log first, yet Maria demands he explain how he
knows a hidden entry exists.'

### 3. Rules for result

- result records the concrete outcome of the scene's actions.
- State what changed relative to the starting position: the characters'
  knowledge, their relationships, available resources, intentions, their
  position in the conflict, or the level of danger.
- The result must follow logically from the action_chain events.
- Do not repeat a brief recap of the action_chain. Record the final state.
- One scene's result must lay the logical groundwork for the next scene.
- Do not end every scene with a mandatory twist. The change may be gradual.

### 4. Distributing word_target across scenes

- Every chapter consists of exactly 5 scenes.
- The chapter-level word_target is copied from input.book_spec.chapter_word_target.
- Each scene's word_target follows from its dramatic weight and the
  complexity of its events.
- The combined word_target of the five scenes must match the chapter's
  target word_target. Allowed deviation — no more than 5%.
- For chapters of 2,500–3,000 words, a scene budget of roughly 400–700 words
  is a good guide.
- Do not distribute the volume mechanically and evenly. Scenes with more
  interactions, complications, and important plot events may take more
  space; transitional and preparatory scenes may be shorter.
- Do not inflate the number of events to reach the volume. Volume follows
  the content and the scene's dramatic necessity.

### 5. Character and world development

- Reveal character through concrete actions, reactions, decisions, mistakes,
  fears, contradictions, and interactions.
- Characters must show individuality through behavior, not through abstract
  traits.
- Include events that let the reader learn how the world works — its rules,
  limits, and peculiarities.
- Reveal world information through actions, consequences, observations, and
  interactions. Do not turn scenes into reference descriptions.
- Account for what the characters already know. Do not make them re-learn
  what they discovered in earlier chapters.
- Show how relationships and behavior change across the novel.
- Do not introduce new abilities, knowledge, objects, or resources unless
  their appearance is justified by the work's material.
- Do not let characters act against their established traits, knowledge, and
  capabilities without a plot-justified reason.

### 6. Scene and chapter coherence

- The five scenes of one chapter form a single sequence of events.
- Each next scene must follow logically from the previous one, but it does
  not have to happen in the same location or with the same participants.
- Do not repeat the same events, conflicts, or revelations within a chapter.
- Account for the consequences of the previous chapters' events.
- Characters' decisions and actions must influence later chapters.
- Do not treat chapters as independent short stories.
- Follow the overall story arc, the central conflict's development, the act
  structure, and the tension_curve.
- Do not reveal secrets before the moment the structure provides for.
- New subplots may appear only when the overall structure justifies them.

### 7. Chapter ending

- The fifth scene always closes the chapter.
- The ending may carry a plot hook, a reversal, an unexpected consequence, or
  an emotional settling.
- Consult the tension_curve: after peak chapters, give the reader room to
  breathe.
- Do not end every chapter on a cliffhanger.
- Alternate ending types according to the novel's dramaturgy.
- The chapter ending must create a natural basis for the next chapter, even
  if it closes on a quiet beat.

### 8. No literary execution inside beats

- beats is an event plan, not finished prose.
- No literary descriptions, metaphors, similes, or ornamental turns of phrase.
- Do not elaborate landscapes, interiors, appearances, or atmosphere unless
  these details matter directly to the action.
- Do not write ready-made dialogue or inner monologue.
- Do not describe facial expressions, gestures, or movements in detail unless
  they convey a meaningful reaction or affect the events.
- Do not fill action_chain with decorative details.
- Focus on actions, intentions, obstacles, interactions, decisions, and
  consequences.

Rules for pov and characters_present:

- characters_present: only ids from bible.characters. No invented ones.
